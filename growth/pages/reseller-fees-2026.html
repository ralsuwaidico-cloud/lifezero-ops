Five marketplaces, five completely different fee structures, and most of them charge on money you never really keep. Here is what each one actually takes in 2026, and where the maths surprises people.

Four things that catch people out

1. "Free shipping" is not free, and you are taxed on it twice over

eBay, Mercari, Depop and Facebook all calculate their percentage on the buyer's total, not on your item price. Charge $8 shipping on a $40 item and eBay's 13.6% applies to $48, not $40. You pay the fee on the shipping money and then you pay for the label. Poshmark is the exception — the buyer pays shipping directly, so it never touches your fee.

2. Poshmark has a cliff at $15, not a slope

At $14.99 you pay a flat $2.95 — about 20% — and keep $12.04. At $15.00 you pay 20% of $15.00, which is $3.00, and keep $12.00. Almost identical. But at $10.00 you still pay $2.95, which is 29.5%. Under about $12, Poshmark is the most expensive place on this list by a wide margin, and listing at $15 instead of $12 can leave you with more money, not less.

3. Facebook Marketplace doubled its fee and a lot of guides never updated

On 15 April 2024 the shipped-order fee went from 5% (minimum $0.40) to 10% (minimum $0.80). Any calculator still showing 5% is telling you that you keep twice as much as you do. Local pickup remains genuinely free.

4. Depop's commission moved to the buyer, not away

Depop dropped its 10% US selling commission in 2024 — for sellers that is a real cut, and on paper it is now the cheapest of the five. What remains on you is 3.3% + $0.45 processing. The catch is that the cost moved to the buyer's side of the transaction, so the same item has to carry a higher sticker price to look comparable. And a Boosted Listing costs 12% on top if you opt into one.

$7.07 of profit separates the best from the worst on a single item. Over a hundred sales that is $707 that depended entirely on where you listed. This is not an argument for abandoning Poshmark — it sells certain brands faster than anywhere else, and a fast sale at 20% beats a slow one at 10%. It is an argument for knowing the number before you list, rather than after.

What is not in these rates

Promoted listings and ad fees (eBay's promoted rate is set per listing and can add several percent), store subscriptions, international and currency fees, Depop and eBay boosts, and the cost of returns. Add those to your own numbers — none of the five includes them in the headline rate.
