Who has to file

Every UAE Corporate Tax registrant files a return for each tax period — companies, free-zone entities, and sole traders/freelancers whose business turnover exceeded AED 1,000,000 in a calendar year. You file even if your tax is zero and even if you claim Small Business Relief. A nil return is still a return.

If you're a freelancer under AED 1M turnover from business activity, you generally don't need to register or file. Salary, personal investments and personal property income don't count toward the AED 1M test.

The date

The return and the payment are due 9 months after the end of your financial year. For the most common case — a financial year ending 31 December 2025 — that is Tuesday 30 September 2026. If your year ends in March or June, your date is different; don't copy someone else's deadline.

Filing and paying share the same deadline. There is no separate later payment window.

What being late actually costs

  • Late return: AED 500 for every month (or part of a month) for the first 12 months, then AED 1,000 a month after that. One day late = a full month. (Cabinet Decision 75 of 2023)
  • Late payment: 14% per year on the unpaid tax, charged monthly — on top of the filing penalty.
  • Never registered when you should have: AED 10,000. Natural persons who crossed AED 1M turnover in a year must register by 31 March of the following year.

Small Business Relief — the thing most small filers should check first

If your revenue is AED 3,000,000 or less in this tax period and in every previous one, you can elect Small Business Relief and be treated as having no taxable income: no Corporate Tax to pay, and a simplified return.

Three things people get wrong:

  1. It is not automatic. You elect it inside the return, and you elect it again every period. Submit without ticking it and you've filed a full return for that year.
  2. It's a revenue test, not a profit test. AED 3M of sales, before any expenses.
  3. The window was just extended. It originally ended with tax periods ending on or before 31 December 2026. Ministerial Decision 131 of 2026 (issued 29 July 2026) extended it to periods ending on or before 31 December 2029. Some older guides — and, until today, our own tracker — still say 2026.

Qualifying Free Zone Persons and members of large multinational groups can't use it.

Worked examples (9% above AED 375,000 taxable income)

  • Revenue AED 600,000, deductible expenses AED 250,000 → taxable income AED 350,000 → below the AED 375,000 band → AED 0 tax. You still file.
  • Revenue AED 900,000, expenses AED 400,000 → taxable income AED 500,000 → (500,000 − 375,000) × 9% = AED 11,250.
  • Revenue AED 2,400,000 (any profit) and you elect Small Business Relief → taxable income treated as nil → AED 0, simplified return.

Six things to do before 30 September

  1. Confirm your own deadline from your financial-year end, not from a headline.
  2. Close the books for the year: every invoice issued, every expense with a receipt, bank reconciled to the last day of the period.
  3. Total your revenue and decide on Small Business Relief now, not inside the EmaraTax form under time pressure.
  4. Separate deductible from non-deductible spend (fines, most entertainment, personal costs are out).
  5. Compute the estimate so the payment is sitting in the account before the day, not found on the day.
  6. File on EmaraTax and pay via GIBAN, then download the acknowledgement and keep it with your records for 7 years.
