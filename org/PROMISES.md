# What this company has told the chairman it would do

The chat panel in the office has no memory. A promise made there — *"I'll come back to you with a
clean answer"* — used to vanish the moment the panel closed, and the chairman spent days waiting on
answers that were never coming (KB-176).

Promises now live as rows in the office page's own store, under `promises`, written the moment an
agent makes one and shown to him under the conversation until they are answered. **This file is the
mirror of that store**, refreshed by the CEO cycle, so that the promise survives even if the page
does not — and so `scripts/liveness.py` can raise an alarm when nobody has been back.

Read the live rows with the `ArtifactData` tool: `action: "list"`, `collection: "promises"`,
`url: https://claude.ai/artifact/D7owTQn2j6KEvPYkMXwyiN`.

## Answered

**2026-09-30 — Can an anime-photo upload product be listed free the way our current products are,
or does it genuinely need new accounts and money?** *(promised in chat before promises were
recorded; answered once the chairman pointed out that promises here went nowhere)*

No, and the reason generalises beyond this idea. All nine of our products are **files**: someone buys
one, downloads it, and serving the next customer costs nothing. An anime-photo product is the
opposite — a customer uploads a photo and something generates a new image for that one person, every
time, including for people who never pay. Our entire automation budget is **$5 a month on a free
plan, one cent used**, and no image model is reachable without a paid account and a card.
Print-on-demand adds an account, a payments setup and shipping on top.

So it is not a small ask. It would be this company's **first product with a cost per customer**,
which is a different kind of business, not a tenth item on the shelf. The recommendation is not to
take it on while nine zero-cost products have never been put in front of one person who was looking
for them. If that changes and we sell a file to a stranger, a paid-per-use product becomes worth the
accounts — not before.
